Open any long-running to-do list and you will find the same thing: forty items,
six of which have been there for a year. The list is not broken. It is just
doing a different job than the one you need.

A list is an **inventory**. A plan is a **commitment**. Confusing the two is why
you can work all day and still feel behind.

## The symptom

You know you are treating a list as a plan when:

- Every morning starts by re-reading everything to decide what to do.
- Items get re-prioritised more often than they get finished.
- Finishing something feels neutral, because the list is still just as long.

## Separate the two surfaces

Keep the inventory. It is genuinely useful — it is where things go so you can
stop holding them in your head. But add a second, much smaller surface that
holds only today.

| Surface | Holds | Size | Reviewed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inventory | Everything you might do | Unbounded | Weekly |
| Today | What you are committing to | 3 items | Each morning |

Three items. Not five, and definitely not "everything that's urgent". The
constraint is the whole point: if a fourth thing gets added, something has to
come off, and that trade is the actual planning decision.

## The hard part

Moving an item from inventory to today means saying the other thirty-seven are
not happening today. That is uncomfortable, which is why most people skip it and
let urgency decide instead.

Urgency is a fine scheduler for emergencies and a terrible one for everything
else — it systematically picks whatever is loudest, which is almost never what
matters most.

## Pruning is part of the work

Once a week, delete things. Not "defer" — delete. An item that has survived
three reviews without being picked is telling you something, and the honest
response is to let it go rather than carry it for another quarter.
