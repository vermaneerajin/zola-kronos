Email, reviews, approvals, the three Slack threads that need a real answer —
this is not busywork, it is the connective tissue of a job. It only becomes a
problem when it is spread evenly across eight hours, so that no hour is ever
fully yours.

The move is not to do less of it. It is to do it in a lump.

## Two or three windows, not forty

Pick two windows — say 11:30 and 16:00 — and let the shallow work pool between
them. Thirty minutes each is usually plenty, and the pooling itself saves time:
half the threads resolve themselves before you get there, and related items
batch naturally because you see them side by side.

The cost is latency. Someone waits two hours instead of five minutes. In most
jobs that is an entirely acceptable trade, and the honest version of the
objection is not "the business needs it" but "I feel rude".

## Set the expectation once

The thing that makes batching stick is not discipline, it is telling people:

> I check messages around 11:30 and 16:00. If something is genuinely urgent,
> call me — I will always pick up.

Giving people a real escape hatch is what makes the rest of it tolerable. In
practice almost nobody uses it, which is itself informative about how much of
the traffic was ever urgent.

## Watch for the drift

Batching decays quietly. You open the inbox "just to check" while a test runs,
and within two weeks you are back to continuous partial attention.

The tell is the reflex: reaching for the inbox during a ten-second wait. That
reflex is the thing to notice — not to scold yourself about, just to notice,
because once you can see it you can leave the tab closed.
